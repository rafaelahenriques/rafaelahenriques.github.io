## What is a Google Doodle

A Google Doodle is a temporary change to the logo on Google’s homepage that marks a holiday, an event or a person. The first one appeared in 1998, when Google’s founders added a Burning Man figure to the logo to tell users they were away. The first animated Doodle was made for Halloween in 2000.

## Why I made this

I made the first version for a motion class in 2024. A Doodle should be built around the logo, but in mine the logo only appeared in the last seconds, cropped at the top and dark against a dark sky. The elements also appeared one after another with no link between them. In 2026, I reworked it to fix these problems and include it in my portfolio.

## What inspired me

My main references were The Nightmare Before Christmas and Coraline. Both are stop-motion films directed by Henry Selick, and both use frightening images in stories for a family audience. A Doodle has a similar audience because anyone can open Google, including children, so it needed to feel spooky without being too scary.

## The art

Most of the artwork comes from my original Photoshop file. It looked dull because of its olive-brown palette, so the layers were recoloured instead of redrawn, which kept my brush texture. Some pieces were repainted to fit the proportions of the logo. The letters are made from unrelated objects, so colour is what connects them as one word. The background was recoloured to violet and teal to keep Google’s four colours clear.

## The motion

The first build of the rework had visible jumps. Each character pose had a different anchor point, which moved the character up to 85 px between frames. Anchoring every pose at the feet and crossfading between poses reduced the largest jump to 15 px.

## The sound

The animation was final before sound was added, so the music had to fit it. The waltz has a strong beat about every 1.32 seconds, and starting it 0.57 seconds into the track places two of those beats on the gravestone’s rise and the caw. The caw is a creature recording instead of a real crow call, which makes the moment more unsettling.
