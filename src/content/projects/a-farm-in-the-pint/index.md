## The concept

The starting point was a Chunks for a Change poster: a raised fist holding a spoonful of ice cream. A protest symbol meets a product, making a point about Fairtrade without losing its humour. That balance is what makes Ben & Jerry’s interesting to me.

Food brands often use farm imagery to communicate product origins. I wanted to take that idea in a less expected direction. Instead of showing how a farm produces ice cream, the ice cream becomes the farm.

I already had a farm diorama to work with, making Ben & Jerry’s a natural fit for the concept.

## Why now

Since 2023, brands such as Jacquemus and Maybelline have used impossible CGI scenes to capture attention on social media. The appeal is straightforward: people stop scrolling to look at something they know cannot be real. But the format has also faced criticism for presenting fabricated scenes as actual events.

This concept makes no attempt at realism. It is deliberately fantastical, using surprise without asking the viewer to believe what they are seeing.

Most of these spectacles live in social feeds. I wanted to explore how the format could work somewhere more purposeful: a product page, where attention can lead directly to a purchase.

## The brief

Designed for the flavours page, the film had three constraints:

- The pint stays the star. It must remain immediately recognisable.
- The animation supports the content. It sits beside the headline without competing for attention.
- The loop is continuous. Visitors can arrive at any point without noticing a beginning or end.

## Starting point

Having an existing diorama changed how I approached the project. The barn, trees, tractor, hay and sunflowers were already built, leaving more room to develop the concept and create the elements it needed: the pint, ice cream and cows.

The challenge was deciding what to keep, what to remove and what the scene actually needed.

## Appetite

Food needs to look good enough to eat before it can be clever.

The ice cream surface went through several iterations, from something resembling sand to whipped cream and soft clay. What finally worked was recreating the way real ice cream tears under a scoop, leaving irregular, scraped layers behind.

The chocolate needed the same attention. My first attempts looked like soft brown lumps. Replacing them with flat-sided pieces, as if snapped from a chocolate bar, made them immediately more recognisable.

The scoop became the hero moment. I made it larger, rounder and gave it more screen time, allowing the food to hold attention before the scene moved on.

## Editing the farm

A diorama rewards close inspection. A web hero needs to communicate almost instantly.

I removed a house, windmill, chicken coop, chickens, pumpkins and a pig sitting in a puddle of chocolate. The pig was probably the funniest idea in the project, but it distracted from the concept. For a dairy-focused scene, a cow made more sense.

Every remaining element had a purpose. The barn communicates the farm setting immediately. The fudge ripple doubles as a path, guiding the eye from the pint towards the barn. Yellow sunflowers add contrast against the brand’s blue, while the sloping mound frames the scene like a miniature theatre stage.

## The cows

The cows needed to feel like Ben & Jerry’s cows, not generic farm animals.

Since 1983, the brand has used Vermont artist Woody Jackson’s distinctive Holstein paintings. I used that graphic character as a starting point, favouring simplified shapes and blocky patches over realistic anatomy or fur.

Their movement was equally important. Each cow grazes independently, with slightly different timing to avoid the mechanical feel of a herd moving in unison.

## On the page

The film shares its space with a headline, so it needs to hold attention without demanding it.

I kept the animation to one continuous shot, with no cuts, allowing the movement to remain calm alongside the text. The background colour comes directly from the flavours page, helping the video blend into the layout without a visible frame.

I also disabled the software’s cinematic colour transform to maintain consistency between the render, the website and the brand palette.

## Where it could go

A flavours page brings together multiple products. The same format could extend across the range, giving each pint its own miniature world while maintaining a consistent visual language: a recognisable pack, a continuous loop and a scene inspired by the flavour.
